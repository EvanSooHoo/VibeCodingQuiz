The conch in Lord of the Flies symbolizes order and structured society. Its presence provides the boys a way to establish rules and hold meetings. As the conch loses its influence, it parallels the breakdown of civilization on the island. Eventually, when the conch is destroyed, it marks the complete descent into savagery. The symbolism in Lord Of The Flies, as is the case for virtually any piece of literature, is subject to debate. This can be substantiated by the effect Ralph (who represents the ego, and not the id) has when using the conch. That being said, virtually no novel of value is restricted to just one interpretation.

Responding to prompt: What is the book Fahrenheit 451 attempting to say? In other words, what is the meaning of the work as a whole?
Option A:

Fahrenheit 451 warns about the dangers of censorship and the loss of intellectual freedom. In the society depicted, books are banned, and independent thinking is discouraged. This suppression creates a population that is easily manipulated. The novel highlights the importance of knowledge and personal connection. Ultimately, it promotes resistance against societal control through education and awareness.

Option B:

The meaning of the novel Fahrenheit 451 is debatable, but the author's actual intent was to convey feelings he had regarding television. Though it is valid to argue that he was cautioning against the detrimental effects of book-burning and censorship, another interpretation is that the novel criticizes entertainment, mass media, and a dystopian society incapable of thorough and independent thought. This is directly demonstrated by the book's depiction of televisions, a hedonistic pursuit of media entertainment to distract from an impending war, and general disdain for intellectuals and intellectual discourse of any kind.
